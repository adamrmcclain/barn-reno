The Concept

Keep the double doors as the focal "wow" entry with screens behind them for summer nights. The bar goes on the left or back wall of the front room. Lounge seating faces the doors to pull the outdoors in. A partition — either a salvaged wood half-wall or a curtain — separates entertaining from storage.

Structure Looks Sound

The barn board exterior, metal roof, and stone foundation appear solid in the photos. Main work is interior: cleaning, flooring, lighting, insulation (optional), and furnishing.

Things to Address Early

Foundation & Floor

The stone foundation looks partially open/deteriorated in the photos — get eyes on that before investing in finishes. The wood floor planks look salvageable but need inspection for rot, especially near the doors.

Check Local Permits

Converting a barn/outbuilding to a recreational/habitable space often requires a permit, especially if adding electricity or plumbing. Worth a quick call to your municipality before starting Plan B or C.

Pest & Critter Check

Any structure like this that's been sitting a while may have mice, bats, or wasps in the walls or rafters. Clear this before doing any insulation or drywall work.

Preserve What's Special

The stained glass panels in the double doors are genuinely beautiful — don't replace them. The barn board walls, if cleaned, will look incredible with proper lighting. The rough wood is the design; don't fight it.

Best Bang-for-Buck Moves

String lights + pendant fixtures do 80% of the atmosphere work. A good area rug grounds the lounge zone. A simple DIY bar (even two sawhorses + a slab of butcher block) signals intent immediately. Prioritize these before anything else.

Screen Solution

For the double doors: a simple screen door insert that swings with the existing doors, or a custom wood-framed screen panel that fits inside the doorframe, will let you keep the doors open on summer nights without bugs. Budget $150–$400 DIY.
