Five zones in 186 sq ft

The key to making a mixed storage space work is treating each category as its own dedicated zone — not mixing them. Once everything has a home, it stays organized with almost no effort. The layout below works with your actual wall positions, the partition opening, and the single door.

🚲 Zone A — Bikes

Right wall · full height

Wall-mounted bike hooks along the right wall, stacked vertically to use the full ceiling height. Keeps bikes completely off the floor — reclaims ~30 sq ft of floor space.

  • 4–6 bikes on hooks
  • Helmets on a small shelf above
  • Pump + repair kit in a bin below

❄️ Zone B — Seasonal

Back wall (window wall) · floor to mid height

The back wall under and beside the windows. Sleds, holiday bins, camping gear — stuff you reach for a few times a year. Low-priority access, so it goes against the farthest wall.

  • Sleds (hang on wall hooks)
  • Labeled bins on shelving
  • Camping / outdoor gear

🔧 Zone C — Tools

Left wall · near partition opening

Left wall closest to the partition opening — tools you reach for regularly. Pegboard panel on the wall for hand tools. Shelving below for power tools, the pressure washer, cords.

  • Pegboard for hand tools
  • Lower shelf for power tools
  • Pressure washer + hoses
  • Extension cords

⚽ Zone D — Sports gear

Left wall · center section

Ball bins, rackets, bats, cleats — the stuff that gets grabbed most often. Open bins at reachable height, right near the door. Easy in, easy out.

  • Ball bin (open top)
  • Rackets / bats vertical
  • Gear bags on hooks
  • Cleats / sports shoes bin

📦 Zone E — Overflow / flex

Center floor · near single door

A small open floor area near the single door for things in active rotation — the stuff that's coming and going. Keep this zone intentionally small to prevent it becoming a catch-all.

  • Lawn mower / seasonal equip
  • Anything currently "in use"
  • Max 4×4 ft footprint
The single door placement works in your favor. With the door on the right wall (storage side), bikes on that wall and sports gear on the left means you enter and immediately access what you need most without walking through anything else.

The sort session — before a single shelf goes up

Everything out of the storage half completely. Sort into four piles. This takes half a day but it's the most important step — you can't design storage for stuff you haven't categorized yet.

Trash / toss

Broken, expired, rusted through, hasn't been used in 3+ years. Be ruthless. Haul-away day or dumpster rental. You cannot organize clutter.

Donate / sell

Working condition but not needed. Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, or a neighborhood group. Kids outgrown bikes are particularly easy to sell fast.

Barn storage

Everything coming back in. Sort by zone (A–E) as you go — don't just make one pile. Box and label seasonal items now, before they go back in.

Elsewhere

Stuff that belongs in the house, garage, or another location entirely. Don't let it creep back into the barn because it has nowhere else to go yet.

!
Count your bikes before buying hooks
5 min
You've got at least 4 bikes visible in the photos, possibly more once the space is cleared. Count them, measure the fattest tire width, and note if any are kids bikes that'll be outgrown soon (those go on the lowest hooks for easy swap). This determines exactly how many hooks and how much wall space Zone A needs.
TIP
Label seasonal bins during the sort — not after
The moment to label a bin is when you're putting things in it, not after it's on the shelf. Have a label maker or masking tape + marker on hand during the sort. Categories that work well for a typical family:
  • Winter / sledding gear
  • Pool / water toys
  • Camping
  • Holiday decorations (by holiday)
  • Sports — offseason gear
DO
Take an inventory photo of each zone's contents
10 min
Before anything goes back in, photograph each pile. Sounds unnecessary but it's very useful when you're at the hardware store trying to remember how many bikes you have or whether the ball bin needs to be 18" or 24" wide. One photo per zone, saved to an album.

Shelving — three different systems for three different zones

One shelving system doesn't fit all the zones. The tool wall, the center unit, and the seasonal back wall all have different requirements. Here's the right approach for each.

Left wall — tools (Zone C)

A 4×4 ft sheet of pegboard mounted to the wall studs is the entire tool storage system. Add pegboard hooks and you can hang every hand tool, cord, and small item with full visibility and easy access. Below the pegboard: one or two deep shelves (2×10 boards on heavy-duty brackets) for the pressure washer and power tools.

  • 4×4 pegboard panel — $20–30
  • Pegboard hook assortment — $15–25
  • 2 heavy-duty shelf brackets + 2×10 board

Center — labeled bins (Zones B+D overflow)

A simple 5-shelf freestanding unit 36–48" wide, positioned so it's visible through the partition opening. This is the unit that needs to look clean — use matching bins with clear labels. The IKEA IVAR or a similar pine shelving unit stained to match the barn is the right call here — looks intentional, not like a garage.

  • IKEA IVAR (pine, adjustable) — $60–90
  • Or build from 1×10 reclaimed — $20–50
  • 6–8 matching bins — $40–70

Back wall — seasonal (Zone B)

Low shelving (max 36" tall) under the windows so the light stays. Two wide shelves for tubs and bins. Sleds, bodyboards, and long items hang above on wall hooks — keeping them vertical saves enormous floor space.

  • 2× 8ft wall-mounted shelves (2×10 on brackets)
  • Heavy-duty wall hooks for sleds/boards
  • Keep below window sill height

Left wall lower — sports (Zone D)

An open-top ball bin (a large wire or plastic bin, or a DIY wooden crate) at floor level for balls and loose gear. Above it: 2–3 hooks for gear bags, backpacks, and racket bags. One shelf for shoes/cleats. This zone needs to be easy access — no latches, no lids.

  • Open wire bin or DIY crate — $15–30
  • 3–4 heavy hooks on board — $15–20
  • One shelf at 48" height for bags

Building the center shelving unit

DO
Stain and seal any pine or new wood to match the barn
Before assembly
An off-the-shelf pine shelving unit sitting in a weathered barn will look completely out of place unless it's finished to match. A coat of your interior stain (dark walnut or whatever you chose) on the IVAR before assembly takes 30 minutes and makes it look like it belongs. No need to match exactly — just get it in the same tonal family.
TIP
Use the French cleat system here too
If you built a French cleat for the bar shelving, run a longer cleat section across the left wall at multiple heights. You can hang pegboard, shelves, bike hooks, and tool holders all off the same cleat system — everything is repositionable, and you only drill into the studs once.
DO
Anchor all freestanding units to the wall
Safety
Any shelving unit over 48" tall needs to be wall-anchored, especially with kids around. A simple L-bracket screwed to the top rear of the unit and into a stud is enough. Anti-tip furniture straps work well too. Takes 5 minutes and prevents a serious hazard. Anti-tip furniture strap — $8–12 L-bracket + 3" screw into stud

Bike storage — off the floor, all of them

Bikes on the floor are the single biggest space-waster in any storage area. Four bikes lying around consume 40+ sq ft. On wall hooks, they consume about 8 sq ft. This is the highest-impact single change you can make in the storage half.

DO
Horizontal wall hooks — simplest and cheapest
Right wall · ~1 hr install
A single rubber-coated J-hook or two-prong bike hook screwed into a wall stud holds one bike by the wheel. Hang front wheel up for a smaller footprint. Stagger heights so handlebars don't clash — alternate left-right, high-low.
  • Space hooks 12–16" apart horizontally
  • Stagger heights: 60" / 48" / 60" / 48" alternating
  • Kids bikes on lower hooks (36–42") for independence
  • Heavier adult bikes on lower hooks for easier lifting
Rubbermaid FastTrack bike hook — $12–18 each Gladiator GearTrack bike hook — $15–20 each
DO
Mount on a horizontal board, not direct to barn wall
Important for old walls
Old barn board walls may not have studs where you need them for bike hook spacing. The solution: mount a 2×8 or 2×10 horizontal board (a "cleat board") directly into the studs across the full right wall, then screw bike hooks anywhere into that board. Gives you full flexibility on hook placement.
  • Run a 2×8 at 54" height across the right wall
  • Hit every stud — minimum 3 stud connections for the weight
  • Hang all hooks from this board, not from the barn wall directly
  • Can double as the top of Zone A and hold a small helmet shelf above
TIP
Add a small shelf above the hooks for helmets + pumps
A single shelf at 72–78" (above the highest bike hook) holds helmets, a floor pump, patch kit, and lock. Everything for the bikes in one zone, nothing on the floor. A row of 3–4 small hooks under the shelf holds helmets if the shelf feels crowded.
!
Check bike tire condition before hanging
A bike with a flat or cracked tire hung on the wall for months is a bike that won't get used when you want it. Quick check before hanging — if a tire needs air or replacing, do it now while the bike is accessible. Takes 10 minutes and avoids a frustrating moment later.

The bin system — labeled, consistent, easy to use

The difference between a storage space that stays organized and one that reverts to chaos in six months is almost entirely the bin system. Consistent bins with clear labels make it frictionless to put things back correctly.

TIP
Use one bin brand, one color family
Matching bins make the space look intentional and organized rather than accumulated. Sterilite or HDX totes from Home Depot in gray or clear are cheap, stack well, and look clean. Buy them all at once in two sizes: 18 gal (most things) and 27 gal (bulky seasonal). Don't mix brands if you can avoid it — different dimensions don't stack cleanly.

Sterilite 18 gal — ~$8 each Sterilite 27 gal — ~$12 each HDX gray totes (Home Depot house brand)
DO
Label on the front face AND the short end
Label system
Bins on a shelf are usually seen from the front. Bins stacked on the floor or in a corner are seen from the end. Label both. A Brother P-Touch label maker produces clean, durable labels — worth the $25 investment for a space like this. Hand-written masking tape labels will smear and fall off within a season.

Brother P-Touch PTM95 — $25 Or chalkboard label stickers + chalk marker
TIP
Put a master list on the wall near the partition opening
A small chalkboard or printed card mounted near the partition opening listing what's in each zone ("Bikes · right wall / Sports · left wall / Seasonal · back") means anyone can find anything without opening every bin. Takes 10 minutes to make and gets used constantly.
