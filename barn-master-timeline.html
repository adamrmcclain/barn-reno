These are the hard sequencing rules — things that cannot be reordered without causing problems. Everything else is flexible.
Cleanout must come before Interior cleaning
You cannot clean or stain around stuff. Everything out first, no exceptions. Trying to work around clutter means uneven cleaning and you'll miss problem areas.
Interior floor cleaning must come before Staining
Stain applied to a dirty or damp surface won't penetrate evenly and will peel. 48–72 hours of dry time after cleaning before any stain touches the wood.
Staining must come before Sealing / poly
Always stain before sealing — sealer locks in the color and protects it. Reversing this order means the stain can't penetrate.
Floor poly — 7-day cure must come before Any furniture or shelving
Uncured poly scratches and scuffs immediately under load. 7 full days before anything heavy sits on it. Non-negotiable.
Roof work must come before Exterior wall staining
Any rust remover, sealant, or coating dripping down from the roof will contaminate freshly stained walls. Always roof first, walls second.
Bar frame must come before Bar top mounting
The top rests on and attaches to the frame. Build the frame, check it for level and square, then cut and mount the top.
Bar top — sink cut + finish must happen before mounting Top goes on frame
Cut the sink hole and finish (stain + seal) the top while it's flat on sawhorses. Doing either operation after mounting is awkward and risks damaging the frame or the finished walls.
Water system test must come before Face boards go on
Test every connection in the RV water system — pump, tubing, faucet, drain — with the underside fully accessible. Discovering a leak after the face boards are on means pulling them off again.
Electrical / lighting must come before Furniture placement
Run all cords, mount all fixtures, and hang the TV before furniture comes in. Moving a sofa back out to run a cord behind it is the kind of thing that kills a weekend.
