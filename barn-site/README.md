# Backyard Barn Renovation — Project Guide

A complete renovation plan for a historic backyard barn, converting it into a rustic entertaining space with a craft/workshop, organized storage, and a stone basement. Built as a multi-page static site for easy reference and sharing.

---

## The Property

A ~26×15.5 ft two-story barn structure with a stone foundation, corrugated metal roof, barn board exterior, wide-plank wood floors, and distinctive stained glass double doors. The building sits on a sloped lot with mature trees and an exposed limestone basement beneath the rear half.

**Key existing features worth preserving:**
- Stained glass panels in the double entry doors
- Dry-stacked limestone foundation and basement walls
- Original barn board interior and exterior walls
- Wide-plank wood floors throughout
- Handmade brick floor in the basement
- Large multi-pane windows in the rear half

---

## The Plan

The building is divided into three distinct zones:

### 1. Entertaining Space (front half — 14 × 15.5 ft)
A rustic hangout with a reclaimed wood bar, lounge seating, wall-mounted TV, and string lights along the rafters. The stained glass double doors open outward to the yard with screen panels behind them for summer use.

**Key features:**
- Reclaimed wood bar with drop-in sink and RV-style portable water system (no plumbing required)
- Full-width bottle shelving with LED underlighting (3 tiers, 8 ft wide)
- 55" wall-mounted TV on the top wall
- Sofa + 2 armchairs facing both the TV and the large window
- Bar stools at the bar
- String lights along exposed rafters
- Screen panels behind double doors for summer nights

### 2. Craft / Workshop (rear half — 12 × 15.5 ft)
Freed from bulk storage by the basement, this half becomes a dedicated craft and workshop space with good natural light from the large rear windows.

**Key features:**
- 8 ft workbench along the back wall (under the windows for natural light)
- Pegboard tool wall on the left side
- Craft table in the center
- Sports gear zone with open ball bin and gear hooks
- Built-in bookcase (existing, stays) used for workshop storage
- Single exterior door on the right wall

### 3. Stone Basement (under rear half — 13 × 13 ft)
A dry-stacked limestone basement with a handmade brick floor and ~7 ft ceiling. Currently open and unused. Becomes weathertight bulk storage for bikes, seasonal bins, and lawn equipment.

**Key features:**
- New exterior door (pre-hung, with deadbolt)
- Three window openings glazed (glass block recommended)
- Brick floor reset, cleaned, and sealed with penetrating masonry sealer
- Dehumidifier with continuous drain for year-round moisture control
- Bike hooks mounted on wall board (all bikes off the floor)
- Heavy-duty metal shelving for labeled seasonal totes
- Wall-mount tool rack for long-handled garden tools
- Stone walls left raw — no paint, no sealer, no parging

## Timeline

At 2 weekends per month, the project runs approximately **7–9 months from cleanout to first gathering**.

| Month | Phase | Key work |
|-------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Prep | Full cleanout both halves, assessment, lead test, stain samples |
| 2 | Interior prep | Wall cleaning (TSP), floor repair, deck cleaner scrub |
| 3 | Interior finish | Stain walls + floor, poly coats, 7-day cure |
| 4 | Storage + Workshop | Shelving, pegboard, bikes, bins, labeled system |
| 5 | Exterior + Roof | Rust treatment, roof coating, pressure wash, wall stain |
| 6 | Bar build | Frame, top, sink, RV water system, shelving, LED strips |
| 7 | Basement | Cleanout, door, windows, floor, dehumidifier, storage |
| 7–8 | Entertaining space | Lighting, TV, screen door, furniture, bar accessories |

**Hard sequencing rules (cannot be reordered):**
- Cleanout before cleaning before staining before sealing
- 48–72 hr dry time after cleaning before any stain
- 7-day poly cure before furniture goes on the floor
- Roof work before exterior wall staining (drips contaminate walls)
- Water system tested before bar face boards go on
- Electrical run before furniture placement

## The Bar — Key Specs

- **Width:** 8 ft (full left wall of entertaining space)
- **Height:** 42" (standard bar height)
- **Depth:** 20" with 8–10" front overhang for stools
- **Construction:** Reclaimed lumber frame, ledger board into studs at 40", front legs, reclaimed face boards
- **Top:** Butcher block or reclaimed wide-plank, pre-finished on sawhorses before mounting
- **Sink:** Drop-in bar sink (stainless, small)
- **Water system:** Shurflo 2088 12V demand pump, 7-gal fresh water tank, RV bar faucet, grey water bucket drain — no plumbing required
- **Shelving:** 3 tiers of 10"-deep reclaimed boards on French cleats, full 8 ft width, LED strip lights underneath
- **Foot rail:** 1.5" black iron pipe on floor flanges

---

## The Basement — Key Notes

- **Walls:** Dry-stacked limestone — do not seal, paint, or parge. Leave raw.
- **Floor:** Old handmade brick — reset heaved bricks, clean with TSP, seal with penetrating masonry sealer only (no film-forming sealer or epoxy — will peel)
- **Moisture:** Check after heavy rain before storing anything. Dry-stacked stone is self-draining but exterior grading matters. Run a dehumidifier year-round at 50–55% RH.
- **Door:** Pre-hung exterior door set into a wood buck frame anchored to the stone with Tapcon masonry screws. Foam, flash, and caulk all gaps.
- **Windows:** Glass block panels (recommended) or fixed single-pane units, each set into a wood buck frame. No casements needed for storage use.
- **No stairs:** The basement has its own exterior door — no interior connection to the upper floor.

## Design Decisions and Notes

**Why RV water instead of plumbing:**
A Shurflo 2088 demand pump, 7-gallon fresh water tank, and grey water bucket provides a fully functional bar sink with zero plumbing, no permits, and about $160–220 in parts. The pump runs on 12V DC — a small rechargeable battery or a wall adapter once electrical is run. Your father-in-law's instinct from his garage build was exactly right.

**Why the workbench is on the back wall:**
The large multi-pane windows on the back wall provide the best natural light in the workshop half — ideal for detailed craft and tool work. Positioning the workbench there also keeps the partition opening completely clear for movement between the two halves.

**Why the stone basement walls stay raw:**
Dry-stacked limestone is self-draining by design — sealing the interior face traps moisture inside the stone and accelerates damage. The stone is also genuinely beautiful once the space is clean and lit. Leave it alone.

**Why glass block for the basement windows:**
Glass block is highly secure (much harder to break than standard glass), requires no operating hardware, provides good light transmission, and installs without a complex frame. For a storage basement, there's no need for openable windows.

**Stain color coordination:**
The interior floor and wall stain should be in the same tonal family but the floor gets full-strength color and the walls get it diluted 20–30%. This creates depth. The exterior wall stain doesn't need to match exactly — a natural cedar or warm brown exterior paired with a darker interior floor is the most common and most successful combination for this building type.
